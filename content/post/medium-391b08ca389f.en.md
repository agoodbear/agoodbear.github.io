### Is this Post. Wellens syndrome or De Winter?

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*G-3NxJGciSjISLUr0SbOOw.png)
*From:[https://ecg-interpretation.blogspot.com/2021/06/ecg-blog-232-47-what-is-bigeminy.html](https://ecg-interpretation.blogspot.com/2021/06/ecg-blog-232-47-what-is-bigeminy.html)*

The latest [ECG blog](https://ecg-interpretation.blogspot.com/2021/06/ecg-blog-232-47-what-is-bigeminy.html)¹ posted an ECG. The ECG seems to show STE in the Inf. wall, plus STD and large T waves in V2~V3. But **Ken Grauer** felt V2~V3 looked like reperfusion after a Post.wall MI, which is why the T waves are tall.

#### When we used to learn ECG, diagnosing a Post.wall MI required a few conditions:

**1.R/S>1 in V1 or V2 (i.e., Tall RV1)
2.STD in V1~V3
3.Upright T waves in V1~V3**

#### Actually, the third point — Upright T waves in V1~V3 — isn't a necessary condition. Why?

Because if there are Upright T waves, it means the Post.wall is showing a reperfusion rhythm: Terminal T wave inversion, so Upright T waves appear in V1~V3 (the mirror image of the Post.wall). Of course, could the Post.wall still be in the middle of a myocardial infarction? Of course!!! And in that case, of course there are no Upright T waves.

Also, in **Ken Grauer**'s ECG, could V2~V3 be **De Winter’s T waves?**

So is this tracing actually a wraparound LAD, rather than an Inf. wall MI + Post.wall MI caused by an RCA problem?

So I wrote an email to **Ken Grauer** to ask. He said he felt the STD in V2 was flat, and he still felt it looked more like the patient had an Inf. wall MI + Post.wall MI, with the Post.wall in the process of reperfusing. He also felt that if these were De Winter’s T waves, they should involve more precordial leads, and would be less likely to be limited to V2~V3.

### Conclusion:

#### Drawing on [**a Smith ECG blog post from a while back**](https://hqmeded-ecg.blogspot.com/2021/03/de-winters-t-waves-are-not-stable-ecg.html) on how to tell De Winter’s T waves from the ST depression of a Post. Wall MI², plus Ken Grauer's reply

**1.How to tell De winter's T waves from Post. Wellens' syndrome ➡ De winter's T waves may evolve from Hyperacute T waves, so the T waves are broad and fat, whereas the Tall T waves from the reperfusion rhythm of a Post.wall MI are the Post.wall's Terminal TWI, so they're narrower
2.If it's De winter's T waves, more Precordial leads are involved**
